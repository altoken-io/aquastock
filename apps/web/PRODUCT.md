# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

AquaStock's public site speaks to two audiences at once: the government or municipal partner considering anchoring a water-infrastructure project, and the community members, diaspora, and outside investors who would co-fund it alongside them. Visitors should immediately understand the core idea — public and private capital funding the same project, on the same table, visible on-chain — and see that this is a hackathon demo, not a live offering.

## Product Purpose

AquaStock is a Solana dApp built for the Stocklana hackathon (NYC) that lets a government "anchor" investor and community/private investors co-fund water-infrastructure projects, with every position, milestone, and impact result tracked on-chain. The public site introduces the project, explains the public/private co-funding model in plain terms, and hands visitors off to the dApp (`apps/dapp`) to connect a wallet and see it working. It is not a marketing site for a live financial product — availability, funding figures, and claims must stay honest to a devnet hackathon build.

## Positioning

The Pentagon became a shareholder in a rare-earths company when the resource mattered enough. AquaStock applies that logic to water: a government becomes the anchor investor in its own water infrastructure, and the community invests alongside it — on one funding table, in the open, on Solana.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Connect a wallet / open the dApp.
- Secondary CTA: See how the public/private funding model works.
- The line a visitor should remember after 10 seconds: One project, one funding table, two kinds of investor — government and community, both on-chain.
- Belief ladder: AquaStock makes public-private infrastructure funding legible; a government position and a community position sit in the same schema, visibly; milestones and impact are verified on-chain, not self-reported; I can connect a wallet and see a real project right now (on devnet).
- Proof on hand: none yet beyond the working demo itself — this is a 5-day hackathon build. Do not claim government partnerships, real funding totals, or production availability that don't exist.

## Brand Personality

Direct, credible, and unshowy. AquaStock should read like serious public-interest infrastructure work, not a token launch — plain numbers, real milestones, no hype.

## Anti-references

Do not make AquaStock feel like a speculative crypto/DeFi project. Avoid token-price framing, yield promises, neon-Web3 visual tropes, and vague "revolutionizing infrastructure" language. This is a transparency and accountability tool for public-private funding, not an investment pitch.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the funding model (public position + private position, one table), not the blockchain mechanics.
2. Make the government/community split visible at a glance — this is the entire point of the product.
3. Never imply more certainty, scale, or real-world adoption than a 5-day hackathon build actually has.
4. Keep the legal reality visible: this is a demo and does not constitute an offer of securities.
5. Build trust through clear on-chain evidence (transaction links, verified milestones) rather than persuasive copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the public web experience, including strong text contrast, keyboard-accessible interactions, visible focus, semantic structure, and readable English and Spanish (the hackathon spec and likely audience are Peru/Latam-facing). Use transitions and animations purposefully, with a reduced-motion alternative that preserves comprehension without unnecessary movement.
